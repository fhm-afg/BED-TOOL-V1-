BED COLOUR TOOL
===============

Double-click "OPEN BED TOOL.bat".

It opens already pointing at your Beds Stock Images, your Colours folders, and
the starter masks. Nothing to set up.


USING IT
--------

  1. Click a bed on the LEFT.
     If a starter mask exists it loads automatically, shown in pink.

  2. Get the pink covering ONLY the upholstery.

     The starter mask covers the WHOLE bed, frame and bedding together. So
     the job is nearly always: take the bedding back out.

     Wand (w)     RIGHT-DRAG across the duvet and pillows. Each drag removes
                  whole regions at a time. Four drags is typical.
                  Left-drag puts a region back.
     Regions      Shows you what the wand sees -- every colour is one click.
                  Worth a look the first time.
     Wand detail  How finely it cuts. HIGHER = smaller, safer regions.
                  Turn it UP if a drag takes too much; DOWN if you are
                  making too many small drags. 12 is a good start.
     Brush (b)    Paint by hand. Eraser (e) or right mouse.
     Size         Slider, or [ and ] keys.
     Undo         Ctrl+Z

     The wand cuts along edges, not colours. That matters on your cream beds:
     the frame and the bedding are near enough the same colour (166 against
     169 brightness) that nothing colour-based can separate them, but the
     seam between them is still a real edge, and the wand stops there.

  3. Save mask (Ctrl+S) when it looks right.
     It reloads automatically next time you open that bed, and the batch
     renderer uses it too. Saved into "Beds Stock Images\_masks".

  4. Click a colour on the RIGHT. The bed recolours instantly.
     Click through the colours freely -- the mask stays put.

  5. Save.
     Save this colour   - just the one on screen
     Save whole fabric  - all 12 / 9 / 11 / 17 of that fabric
     Save every colour  - all 49

     Everything lands in "Beds Stock Images\_recoloured".

  THE THREE SLIDERS (all apply to saves, not just the preview)

    Brightness    - nudge if a colour comes out heavier or lighter than the
                    real fabric.
    Texture       - how much of the fabric's own grain to show.
                    0 = flat colour, 1 = normal, 2 = exaggerated.
    Texture size  - how large the grain is. Smaller number = finer.
                    Around 260 suits the velvets; marble likes 350-450 so
                    the veining does not look like sandpaper.


WHY IT LOOKS RIGHT
------------------

  It keeps the photograph's own light and shade -- the channel tufting, the
  folds, the shadow under the rail -- and swaps only the colour underneath.

  The colour comes from the median of your swatch photo, not the average,
  so the softbox highlight across your swatches does not skew the result.

  The grain comes from the swatch too. Your swatches are close-ups of draped
  fabric, so they carry the photographer's lighting as well as the weave.
  The tool strips out anything that varies slowly (that is the lighting) and
  keeps what varies over a few pixels (that is the fabric), then lets the
  bed's own shading do the lighting instead.

  These photos are small, so at full size the grain is deliberately subtle.
  Push the Texture slider past 1 if you want it more obvious.


DOING A LOT AT ONCE
-------------------

  Once a bed's mask is right, save the whole fabric or all 49 in one click.
  49 colours takes about 4 seconds.

  "RENDER EVERYTHING.bat" does every bed that has a mask, in every colour,
  without opening the window. It prints which beds it skipped and why.

IF SOMETHING BREAKS
-------------------

  "python is not recognised"
      Install Python from python.org, tick "Add python.exe to PATH".

  "No module named cv2" / "PIL" / "numpy"
      Run:  pip install numpy opencv-python Pillow

  A bed opens with no starter mask
      Normal for some. Just brush it.

  Nothing happens when clicking Save
      You need both a mask and a colour selected.


RESOLUTION, WORTH KNOWING
-------------------------

  These stock photos are 223x277 to 421x492 pixels. Output matches the input,
  so it is good for grid cells around 300px -- the size your existing Liana
  and Savoy grids use -- but not for a large hero shot.

  For full-size images, make one high-resolution render per bed first, put it
  in a folder, and point the tool at that folder instead. Every colour then
  comes off one identical source, which is what keeps a grid consistent.
